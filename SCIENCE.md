# Why SlowLight uses this pace

SlowLight is a quiet visual breathing guide for winding down. Its pacing is informed by published research on slow breathing. SlowLight itself has not been clinically tested.

## Why six breaths per minute?

Six breaths per minute means one breath every ten seconds. This is a commonly studied rate: breathing-related changes in heart rate can interact with the body's blood-pressure regulation (the baroreflex). Research reports changes in heart-rate variability and sometimes relaxation. Increased variability during paced breathing is not, by itself, proof of better health or a treatment effect.

[Zaccaro et al. (2018), systematic review of 15 studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30245619/)

## Why four seconds in and six seconds out?

At the final pace, SlowLight uses roughly four seconds inhaling and six seconds exhaling, without a breath hold. This is an unhurried design choice, not a uniquely proven ratio. A randomised trial of 100 healthy adults compared longer-exhale and equal-timing practice over 12 weeks. Both groups improved on stress measures, but there was no statistically significant advantage for the longer exhale. Without a non-breathing control, improvement within the groups cannot establish that breathing alone caused it.

[Birdee et al. (2023), longer versus equal exhalation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36871835/)

## Why use it before bed?

A study of 64 young healthy adults found better self-reported sleep quality after 15 minutes of evening slow breathing daily for 30 days compared with social-media use. That comparison cannot separate the breathing effect from avoiding social media or taking quiet time. It did not test SlowLight.

[Laborde et al. (2019), bedtime breathing study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6406675/)

## What is uncertain?

A larger randomised trial assigned 400 adults to approximately 5.5 or 12 breaths per minute for around ten minutes daily over four weeks. Very slow breathing did not outperform the ordinary-paced breathing comparison on mental health and wellbeing. This makes claims that six breaths per minute is a universally superior or guaranteed calming rate inappropriate.

[Fincham, Strauss and Cavanagh (2023), active-control trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38092805/)

SlowLight's 11-to-6 breaths-per-minute transition over two minutes, its 8- and 20-minute options, colours, screen light and torch cue have not been validated together. The ramp is intended to help users approach the rhythm gradually; the session lengths are practical options rather than proven doses. Six breaths per minute is a research-informed default, not a measurement of your personal resonance frequency. The app does not measure your breathing or heart rate.

## Comfortable practice

Use the light as an optional guide. Breathe gently, without forcing unusually deep breaths or holding your breath. Stop and return to normal breathing if you feel dizzy, breathless or uncomfortable. SlowLight supports general relaxation; it is not a medical device or treatment for insomnia or anxiety.

## What has been verified in the app?

The pacing code specifies a starting rate of 11 breaths per minute, a linear reduction to 6 over 120 seconds, and an inhale/exhale split of 40%/60%. After the ramp, that gives a ten-second cycle with four seconds inhaling and six exhaling. Automated unit tests cover the breathing engine and associated behaviour.

This is software verification: it checks the intended timing logic. It does not measure a user's actual breathing, demonstrate a health outcome, or validate the phone display or torch as a clinical intervention. SlowLight provides no physiological feedback and does not personalise a resonance frequency.

## What would stronger validation require?

A clinical study would need a defined population, appropriate comparison, prespecified outcomes and sufficient participants. Demonstrating that this app improves sleep is a different question from showing that a user finds it pleasant or easy to use. Establishing that a two-minute ramp or a 4:6 ratio is best would require directly comparing those choices.

Our current claim is deliberately narrower: SlowLight makes a research-informed breathing rhythm convenient to follow as part of a relaxation routine.
